    <section class="research-home-section" aria-labelledby="current-research">
      <h2 id="current-research" class="research-section-title">Current and Continuing GPS/PNT Research</h2>

      <p class="research-section-intro">
        Stanford GPS Lab research advances accurate, trustworthy, and resilient position, navigation, and timing. Current and continuing work spans satellite- and ground-based augmentation, transportation safety, alternative PNT, precise timing, inertial sensing, and multi-constellation GNSS. Additional projects apply these technologies to rail systems, marine-animal tracking, and flexible software-defined receivers.
      </p>

      <h2 id="early-research" class="research-section-title">Early GPS/PNT Research</h2>

      <p class="research-section-intro">
        The Lab’s foundational research in the 1990s and early 2000s helped demonstrate new uses of GPS across spacecraft, autonomous aircraft, precision landing, agriculture, integrity monitoring, and terrestrial navigation. This work established many of the ideas that now underpin modern safety-critical PNT systems.
      </p>
