  <h1>Marine Animals Tracking</h1>

  <img src="{{ '/assets/img/research/tuna-tagging-collage.jpg' | relative_url }}"
       alt="Marine Animals Tracking: Tuna Tagging 5-image photo collage strip"
       class="research-content-image">

  <p class="research-intro">
    Stanford's Hopkins Marine Station, led by Professor Barbara Block, has been
    pioneering the use of novel navigation tags to study the migratory behavior of
    large marine animals—especially tuna, sharks, billfish and turtles.
  </p>

  <p>
    The GPS Lab and SCPNT have recently teamed with Hopkins Marine to develop a
    GPS tag that can provide a marine animal's GPS position location in near
    real-time. The tag, when activated, transmits the animal's position using
    satellite phone (satphone) technology. The tag is being developed to detect
    illegal shark fishing. This technology can also be applied to protect and
    track other marine species.
  </p>
